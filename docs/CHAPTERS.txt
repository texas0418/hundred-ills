HUNDRED ILLS - CHAPTER STRUCTURE
Compiled 2026-08-19. Answers: an eight-hour phone game needs stopping
points, without breaking the one-continuous-night illusion.


=============================================================
THE PROBLEM
=============================================================

Phone sessions are twenty to forty minutes. The game is eight hours,
has no map, no HUD, no quest log and no hint system by design. A player
who puts it down at a random moment and comes back four days later has
lost the thread and will not finish.

But a chapter select screen, a "Chapter 3 of 25" card, or a save menu
puts a game-shaped object between the player and the night. The whole
premise is that this is ONE walk.

Both things have to be true. They already are, because the period
solved it.


=============================================================
THE ANSWER - 更點, WATCHES AND POINTS
=============================================================

[A, verify] The night was divided into five watches, 五更, of roughly
two hours each. Each watch was further divided into five 點, points, of
roughly twenty-four minutes. 三更二點 is a real way to say a time. The
phrase 五更三點 turns up in literature.

  5 watches x 5 points = 25 CHAPTERS of about twenty minutes
  25 x 20 minutes = roughly eight hours

READ THAT TWICE. The game has TWENTY-FIVE chapters, not five. Five is
the number of WATCHES, which are ninety-minute acts, and the only
thing that gets a title card. The chapter - the unit a player actually
plays in one sitting and stops at - is the 點, and there are five of
them inside every watch.

Twenty minutes is a phone session. The traditional Chinese night is
already subdivided into exactly the granularity a mobile game needs.
We did not have to invent anything and we do not have to explain
anything.

THEY ARE NOT UNIFORM, and should not be. Real watches were measured by
actual darkness and stretched or shrank with the season. Ours is the
first lunar month - deep winter, the longest nights of the year, which
is itself the justification for a long game. So some points run ten
minutes and some run thirty-five. The average lands the whole night at
six to eight hours per DECISIONS 83, and the unevenness stops the game
feeling gridded.

THE THREE GRIDS, which are independent and should stay that way:

  TIME, ACTS       five watches, 一更 to 五更, ninety minutes each
  TIME, CHAPTERS   five points per watch, twenty minutes each, 25 total
  SPACE            three districts, per DECISIONS 85

Space does not align to time. You will be in the second district across
parts of two watches. That misalignment is good - it stops the game
feeling gridded.


=============================================================
WHO MARKS THEM - 打更人, THE NIGHTWATCHMAN
=============================================================

[A] The 打更 watchman walked the streets all night striking a bamboo
clapper or a small gong, calling the watch and the point aloud so the
town knew the hour.

HE IS THE CHAPTER BREAK. Not a card, not a menu. You hear the clapper
somewhere in the streets, he calls 三更二點, and the game saves.

What this buys, all at once and all diegetic:

  A STOPPING POINT. The player hears a chapter end. They know it is a
  clean place to put the phone down, and nobody had to tell them.

  A CLOCK. DECISIONS 15 wants the night tightening toward cockcrow.
  Now it is audible and it is counting.

  A SAVE. Autosave on his call. No save menu anywhere in the game.

  A PLACE MARKER. Come back four days later and the game says 三更二點
  and you know exactly where you are in the night.

  A HEAT SOURCE. He is a living man walking around outside all night
  carrying a lamp. Per DECISIONS 84 he is a relighting opportunity that
  MOVES, which is mechanically valuable in the dead districts.

  A CHARACTER. He is the only other person out walking. Over eight
  hours he becomes the most familiar presence in the game.

THE BEAT THIS UNLOCKS, and it is free:

Early on he greets her. Nods, says something ordinary about the cold,
calls the watch, moves on. The player registers nothing - a man doing
his job. It quietly establishes that she is seen, that she is alive,
that the world acknowledges her.

Some hours later he walks straight past her.

No music, no comment, no reaction from her. He simply does not see her
and continues calling the hour. The player notices. She does not.

That is a mid-game confirmation of ending B that costs one line of
dialogue removed, and it lands harder than any set piece we could
build.

CAREFUL: he must never be spooky. He is a cold, bored man doing a
tedious job. The moment he is written as an omen he stops working.


=============================================================
MAKING THE BREAK PERCEPTIBLE - the fix
=============================================================

A stopping point the player does not NOTICE is not a stopping point.
An earlier draft of this file marked the points with an ambient clapper
and nothing else, which is too subtle to do the job it exists to do.
The player has to know, without a card and without being told, that a
chapter just ended and this is a clean place to stop.

Three things do it, all diegetic, none of them UI.

1. HE STRIKES THE COUNT. [A, verify] The watchman's strikes encoded the
   hour. Ours does it legibly: the watch as slow deep strikes, then the
   point as quick light ones. 三更二點 is three slow, pause, two quick.

   The player learns to count within the first watch because nobody
   explains it and the number is always right. From then on every
   chapter break announces exactly where you are in the night, in a
   language you taught yourself. It is the game's clock, its chapter
   number, and its progress bar, and it is a man hitting a piece of
   bamboo.

2. THE WORLD HOLDS. For the two or three seconds he is calling, input
   is still live but nothing moves and no other sound plays. Not a cut,
   not a fade - a held breath. It reads unmistakably as a beat ending.

3. THE SAVE IS VISIBLE BY BEING AUDIBLE. Because the strike ALWAYS
   coincides with the autosave, the sound becomes the save indicator
   inside about forty minutes of play. Players will start walking to
   the next clapper before putting the phone down, which is exactly the
   behaviour we want and we never asked for it.

HOW THE BREAK IS PRESENTED

Between points: the strike, the count, the held beat, and the walk
continues unbroken. No fade, no card, no menu, no interruption.

Between WATCHES, five times only: a full stop. Black. The watch name
set large in the app's real type, per DECISIONS 57's no-text-in-images
rule.

        二更
        the second watch

Nothing else on the card. No chapter number, no progress bar, no
percentage, no "continue" button that has to be pressed. It holds for
a few seconds and the night resumes.

Five of these in eight hours - one every ninety minutes, at the act
breaks only. Twenty-five audible chapter breaks, five visible cards.
That is the entire chapter furniture of the game.

RESUME BEHAVIOUR: relaunching drops you back at the last point with the
clapper sounding and the watch called once. That single audio cue is
the whole "previously on" and it is enough.


=============================================================
WHAT IT COSTS THE ENDING
=============================================================

Check ENDING-SEQUENCE.txt against this. The watchman is calling points
all through the finale, which means the last stretch has an audible
countdown running under it - good, and free tension.

But he must be ABSENT for the door sequence in Movement Three. That
scene is a still image, a lamp, a name, and a game that will not tell
you what it wants. A clapper in the distance during it would give the
player something to wait for, and the entire point is that there is
nothing to wait for.

Last call is 五更三點, some minutes before she reaches the door. Then
nothing until cockcrow, which only sounds in ending two.

The silence after his last call is the loudest thing in the game.
