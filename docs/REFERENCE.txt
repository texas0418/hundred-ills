START HERE - the single best free resource
=============================================================

135 woodblock prints: nianhua, door gods, historical figures and
Taoist deities. Metropolitan Museum, accession CP446, 19th-20th
century, polychrome woodblock, ink and colour on paper. Free download.
VERIFIED.

https://archive.org/details/mma_one_hundred_thirtyfive_woodblock_prints_including_new_years_pictures_nian_63878

This is exactly our register: LATE, popular, coarse, cheap village
printing rather than refined 18th-century work. Per ART.txt point 4,
the degraded late-Qing look is the one we want. Pull door gods from
here.

=============================================================
THE HIGH END - what Suzhou printing was before it declined
=============================================================

Cleveland Museum of Art acquired 113 Suzhou colour woodblock prints
from the Christer von der Burg collection. CMA and the Met jointly took
220 Gusu prints. Open access, high resolution. VERIFIED.

https://www.clevelandart.org/articles/18th-century-prints-suzhou

Named works with accession numbers to look up directly in CMA's
collection search:
   Mother and Sons, 1736-95, accession 2025.23
   Bird on Lotus, accession 2025.118
   Plum and Camellia in a Bronze Vase, first half 1700s, 2025.127

CMA collection search, filter to Open Access for downloadable files:
https://www.clevelandart.org/art/collection/search

Why look at these if we are making the coarse late version: because
this is where the perspective and receding planes come from, per
ART.txt point 3. Study the depth, then throw away the refinement.

=============================================================
WHAT TO ACTUALLY LOOK FOR
=============================================================

When you open these, you are calibrating on four things and nothing
else. Ignore subject matter, ignore beauty.

  1. LINE WEIGHT. Even, carved, slightly blunt. Not tapered like a
     brush stroke. This is the single tell that separates a woodcut
     from everything the model wants to give you.

  2. FLATNESS. Every colour is one solid value. Where you think you
     see shading it is almost always a second flat block overprinted.

  3. REGISTRATION. Find prints where the colour sits slightly off its
     outline. That misalignment is our whole health system, per
     DECISIONS 59. Screenshot the good examples and keep them.

  4. PALETTE COUNT. Count the colours. It is usually four or five.
     If a generated plate has eight, it is wrong no matter how nice.
