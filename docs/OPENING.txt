HUNDRED ILLS - THE FIRST FIVE MINUTES
Draft 1, 2026-08-21. The fiction pass begins here. Nothing in this
file is in the app; it is written to be torn apart. Where a line of
player-facing text is proposed, it is quoted and marked [C] like any
other invention (LORE.txt rules apply to fiction too - the almanac
layer stays [A]/[B], but we should know what we made up).

The unit covered is 一更一點, the night's first point - the twenty-odd
minutes from launch to the first strike of the clapper - with the five
minutes to the mooring written beat by beat, and the rest of the point
sketched. ENDING-SITE's six plants are placed; four of the six land
inside the first point.


=============================================================
THE VOICE - the decision everything else waits on
=============================================================

DECISIONS 37 fixes the register: period-plain, no chinoiserie, reads
like a translated primary source. 35 fixes the layout: 繁體 first,
English beneath, smaller. What is not yet fixed is WHOSE English it is.

PROPOSED: two voices only, and the game never explains the difference.

  THE WORLD'S VOICE. Everything the town itself says: the watchman's
  call, the almanac, the fortune poems, a shop sign. Always bilingual,
  characters first. This voice is quotation - half of it literally is.

  HER VOICE. First person, present tense, never more than two short
  sentences at a time, and rare - most screens say nothing. She talks
  the way a tired practical woman talks to herself on an errand. She
  never describes what the player can see. She only says things the
  painting cannot: what she intends, what she remembers, what she
  refuses to think about. English only - it is interior, not text.

  There is NO third voice. No narrator, no "you feel", no stage
  directions. If neither the world nor she would say it, it is not
  said.

The test for every line of hers, per 65: it must read two ways. First
walk: an ordinary woman's ordinary thought. Second walk: the dead
denying what they are. Sample, at the gate (see beat 2):

    "The nail-heads first. Then the walk. Do it properly
     and it counts."

First reading: rite instructions, a practical mind. Second: the exact
voice of someone performing being alive, which is what the fires are
(66). Every line ships only if it survives both readings.


=============================================================
THE FIRST FIVE MINUTES, BEAT BY BEAT
=============================================================

BEAT 0 - COLD OPEN (0:00)

Black. Water sounds, close. One clapper strike, far away, then the
watch card per 36:

    一更
    the first watch

Nothing else. No studio card, no menu - the title screen exists
outside the night (unchanged from current build); this is what NEW
GAME opens into. The card holds while the water grows, then the gate
screen fades in from the mist top-down, the way ink settles.

BEAT 1 - THE GATE, SHUT (0:20)

The gate painting. She stands before the shut studded gate. No text
yet. The player has a still, beautiful, closed image and no
instruction. Whatever they try first - tap, swipe up, swipe left -
either does nothing or refuses with the small haptic. This is
deliberate and it is the game teaching its first lesson: the world
answers, it does not explain.

BEAT 2 - 摸釘 (0:40)

The bronze studs are a touch target - the game's first working input
is a TOUCH on the painting, before movement is ever discovered.
Touching them: a soft metal-cold sound, the faintest warm bloom under
her hand (Skia), and her first line:

    "The nail-heads first. Then the walk. Do it properly
     and it counts."

Then, only after the touch, a second line - the tell at 30, planted
sincerely, the cruelest object per 70:

    "釘 for 丁. A son. Everyone asks for a son."

She already has him. He is why she is dead. The line is warm, hopeful
and unremarkable on first reading, and it is the whole game on the
second. NOTE: whether she should ask for a son AT ALL given she has
one is a real question - see OPEN QUESTION 4 for the alternative
reading where the line is habit, not request.

Touching the studs again does nothing. The rite counts once.

BEAT 3 - THE TURN (1:20)

The only exit is down - she turns from the gate and walks back down
the lane. The player finds this by trying; nothing prompts them. The
first successful swipe is the phase transition itself, which is its
own reward. No text. (The opening move of the game is turning away
from a shut door. Per 18, later, turning BACK has a price - the rite
charges for looking behind. The symmetry is deliberate and never
pointed at.)

BEAT 4 - THE GATE LANE (1:40)

The corridor under the wall. As she enters, far down the lane, the
watchman's clapper again - closer than the cold open. No text. The
screen's job is scale: the wall says the town is large and she is
small and it is late.

BEAT 5 - THE MOORING, AND HER NAME (2:30)

Down the lane's foreground to the mooring. THE WATCHMAN IS HERE -
this is 89's free beat, moved to minute three because it also solves
ENDING-SITE plant 6: her name spoken by someone living, learned the
ordinary way. He is bored, cold, middle-aged. He does not stop
walking.

    WATCHMAN: "Out for the walk, 【NAME】? Bridges are slick."
    HER:      "I'll mind them."

Two lines, the entire exchange. He exits the way she came, clapper
fading. His greeting is the only time tonight a living person speaks
to her - though neither reading of the scene flags it. On the second
walk the player knows why his greeting is impossible and the
exchange becomes the saddest thing in the first watch. (He does not
strike the hour here; his sounding walk pattern belongs to the audio
pass.)

Her name: see OPEN QUESTION 2. Everything else in this file works
around the placeholder.

BEAT 6 - THE WATER (3:30)

The mooring's down-exit to the water's edge is visibly there - dark
steps, black water. Players will try it now or soon; the fires are
taught whenever they do, so the timing is theirs (41: no tutorial).
At the water's edge, the flames render in the reflection per 67 -
three small vermilion points on the black water, the screen's only
red. Her line, the only fires text in the game:

    "Three. Good. Steady the whole way, that's the trick
     of it."

Counting them is HER habit, so it becomes the player's. The game
never says fires, never says three, never says what they are for.
(66: never the word health, never a bar.) If the player never comes
down here, the fires are simply never explained - the almanac's
三把火 entry is the only other source. That is accepted and correct.

BEAT 7 - THE BANK OPENS (4:30)

Back up, and the walk proper: mooring, right, toward bridge A. The
first watch's business begins. Five minutes in, the player knows how
to move, has touched the rite, has heard her name, has maybe seen
the fires, and has been told nothing.


=============================================================
THE REST OF 一更一點 - sketch
=============================================================

The point runs long (CHAPTERS: points are not uniform; make the
first one generous - twenty-five minutes plus). Its business: walk
the near bank end to end, cross bridges A and B in the ordinary
course of it, and place three more plants. The point ends with the
watchman's first full strike (一更二點 - slow strike, two quick),
which is also the first autosave the player might notice.

PLANT 1+2 - THE HOUSE AND THE LAMP (ENDING-SITE 1, 2)

Canal two is reachable from the junction inside the first point, and
the ring makes passing the house natural rather than staged. Passing
house-lamp, its window lit (the painting already keeps the lamp lit
in both ink states - the drain bake preserved it):

    "Lamp's burning at ours. Wasteful. I'll be back
     before it matters."

The mundane reason, delivered as thrift. [C], and see OPEN QUESTION
5 - "ours" does a lot of quiet work here and Simon should decide how
early the house is claimed as hers. She does not stop. Nothing stops
her. The lamp is lit in every later pass and no one ever mentions it
again.

PLANT 4 - THE NEIGHBOUR'S WALL (ENDING-SITE 4)

The wall screen wires in next to the house - graph change proposed
below. First pass line, warm, no music cue, per the brief:

    "The Zhous' new-year print. A fat boy and a carp.
     Theirs came in autumn - a girl, but healthy."

One neighbour, one detail, gone. On the second walk the print of a
thriving baby boy next door to HER house is unbearable, which is the
assignment. ([C]; the surname is set dressing and can change.)

PLANT 5 - THE ALMANAC (ENDING-SITE 5)

Present from minute one by existing (47's companion layer). Nothing
in the first point references it. Its 姑獲鳥 and 血湖 entries are
simply there, dry, per 77. No action in this file; noted so the
plant list reconciles.

PLANT 3 - THE VOICE, ONCE (ENDING-SITE 3)

NOT in the first point. It belongs at the very edge of the first
watch (一更五點, the last minutes before the 二更 card), from behind
her, distant, once:

    【NAME】——

No sting, no fire cost, nothing to answer yet. She does not turn.
One line from her, and this one is load-bearing:

    "No one calls me on the walk. Everyone knows the rule."

Which is 19 taught diegetically - answering has a cost - and is also
false comfort: someone IS calling her, and it is the person who
loves her most (73). Both readings, one line.

=============================================================
OPEN QUESTIONS - answer with numbers
=============================================================

1. THE VOICE. Two-voice scheme as proposed (world + her interior,
   no narrator)? The alternative is Year Walk's near-wordlessness -
   cut her voice entirely, keep only world text. That is purer and
   colder; the two-voice version buys the 65 double-readings and the
   ending's "answer to your name" needs her to have had a voice all
   night. Recommend: two voices.

2. HER NAME. Needed before any line records. Three candidates, all
   plausible for a late-Qing Jiangnan woman of her standing:
     a. 阿秀  A-Xiu   - plain, warm, common; "the pretty one" worn
                        soft by use. Recommended.
     b. 玉娘  Yuniang - a touch more formal; "niang" lands harder
                        when the voice calls it (it also means
                        mother, which 叫魂 will exploit).
     c. 招娣  Zhaodi  - "beckoning a little brother"; a girl named
                        as a wish for a son. On-theme to the point
                        of cruelty - possibly past it.
   Recommend a, with b's 娘-resonance noted as a real argument for b.

3. THE RITE'S RULES - where does the player learn don't-look-back /
   don't-answer? Proposed above: nowhere explicit in the first point;
   her line at plant 3 carries don't-answer, the almanac carries the
   rest, and the first look-back costs a fire whenever the player
   tries it (18: a price, never a wall). The alternative: an epigraph
   on the 一更 card quoting a real 走百病 source ([A] wording pending
   the 帝京景物略 research in LORE). Recommend: no epigraph, rules
   stay diegetic; revisit only if testers flounder.

4. THE 摸釘 LINE. As drafted she asks for a son sincerely (70's
   reading). Alternative: she touches the studs out of habit and
   almost-names the child - "釘 for 丁. Well. I needn't ask this
   year." - which plants the child harder but risks giving the game
   away in minute one. Recommend: as drafted; save the nearness for
   the second walk.

5. "OURS" - the house-lamp line claims the house as hers on first
   pass. Alternative is a neutral "Someone's left a lamp burning all
   night" - colder, hides more, but wastes plant 1's requirement
   that she PASSES HER OWN HOUSE with a mundane reason. Recommend:
   "ours", said once, never again.
